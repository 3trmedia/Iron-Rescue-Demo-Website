Gear We Train With

The Right Equipment Is Half The Battle.

Knowing what to do only matters if you have the right gear in hand when it counts. Here's what we use in class, and what we can get you outfitted with when training ends.

Trauma Gear

What's In The Kit

Every item below is taught hands-on in class before it's ever handed to you as your own.

CAT Gen 7 Tourniquet
The same combat application tourniquet trusted by military medics. You'll learn to apply it one-handed, under stress, in under 60 seconds.
QuikClot Combat Gauze
Hemostatic gauze that accelerates clotting for wounds a tourniquet can't reach. You'll practice packing it correctly on realistic bleeding simulators.
Vented Chest Seal
Prevents a penetrating chest wound from becoming a fatal pneumothorax. One of the most critical, and most overlooked, tools in a trauma kit.
Bleeding Simulators
Realistic wound simulators used throughout every course, so you're practicing on something that behaves like the real thing, not a diagram.
Reecoil Deploy Trauma Kit
A complete, compact IFAK configured for real emergencies. Available bundled with TECC training, or on its own for students who already have their certification.
Trauma Shears & Gloves
The unglamorous essentials. Fast access to the injury and basic protection for the person providing care are non-negotiable in any kit.
How It All Fits Together

The MARCH Algorithm

Every piece of gear in your kit maps directly to a step in MARCH, the same casualty assessment order used by military and tactical medics.

M
Massive Hemorrhage
Tourniquet and hemostatic gauze to stop life-threatening bleeding first, before anything else.
A
Airway
Clear and maintain an open airway so the patient can keep breathing.
R
Respiration
Chest seal applied to any penetrating chest injury to keep lungs functioning.
C
Circulation
Check for further bleeding and signs of shock once the first threats are controlled.
H
Hypothermia / Head
Keep the patient warm and assess for head injury while you wait for help to arrive.
